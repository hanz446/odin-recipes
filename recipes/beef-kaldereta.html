Beef Kaldereta

What is Beef Kaldereta?

Beef Kaldereta (or Caldereta) is a beloved Filipino beef stew renowned for its rich, thick, and savory sauce. Originally heavily influenced by Spanish cuisine, the Filipino version has evolved to feature a unique secret ingredient: liver spread. This addition gives the tomato-based sauce its signature depth, thickness, and slightly earthy flavor. Commonly served during fiestas, holidays, and special family gatherings, it is traditionally cooked slowly until the beef is fork-tender, accompanied by potatoes, carrots, bell peppers, and sometimes green olives or chili peppers for a kick of heat.

Ingredients

The Meat and Base

  • 2 lbs beef chuck or brisket (cut into 2-inch cubes)
  • 3 tbsp cooking oil
  • 1 medium onion (chopped)
  • 4 cloves garlic (minced)

The Sauce

  • 1 cup tomato sauce
  • 1/2 cup liver spread (or canned liver pâté)
  • 2 cups beef broth (or water with a beef bouillon cube)
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 3 dried bay leaves

The Vegetables

  • 2 medium potatoes (peeled and cubed)
  • 2 medium carrots (peeled and sliced)
  • 1 red bell pepper (sliced)
  • 1 green bell pepper (sliced)
  • 1/4 cup pitted green olives (optional)
  • 1-2 Thai chili peppers (chopped, optional for heat)

Seasoning

  • Salt and freshly cracked black pepper to taste

Procedure

  1. Sear the beef: Heat the cooking oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the beef cubes and cook until browned on all sides. Remove the beef and set it aside.
  2. Sauté the aromatics: In the same pot, leave about 2 tablespoons of the beef drippings and oil. Add the chopped onions and garlic. Sauté until the onions are soft and translucent.
  3. Start the stew: Return the browned beef to the pot. Pour in the tomato sauce and beef broth. Add the bay leaves. Bring the mixture to a boil.
  4. Simmer until tender: Once boiling, reduce the heat to low, cover the pot, and let it simmer for 1.5 to 2 hours, or until the beef becomes very tender. Stir occasionally and add a splash of water if the sauce reduces too quickly.
  5. Add the rich flavors: Stir in the liver spread, soy sauce, and chili peppers (if using). Mix well until the liver spread dissolves smoothly into the sauce, allowing it to thicken. Let it simmer for another 5 minutes.
  6. Cook the vegetables: Add the potatoes and carrots. Cover and simmer for about 10 to 12 minutes, or until the vegetables are easily pierced with a fork.
  7. Add the finishing touches: Stir in the red and green bell peppers, along with the green olives. Let it cook for a final 3 to 5 minutes so the peppers soften slightly but retain their crunch.
  8. Season and serve: Taste the sauce and season with salt and pepper as needed. Remove the bay leaves. Serve piping hot with a large bowl of steamed white rice.
