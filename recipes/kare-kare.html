Kare-kare

What is Kare-kare?

Kare-kare is a beloved traditional Filipino stew famous for its rich, thick, and savory peanut sauce. Originally hailing from the culinary capital of Pampanga, it is traditionally made with oxtail, beef tripe, and occasionally beef chuck, slow-cooked until meltingly tender. The vibrant, golden-orange stew is loaded with native vegetables such as banana blossoms, string beans, eggplant, and bok choy. Because the stew itself is relatively mild and unseasoned, it is always paired with a side of salty, umami-packed sautéed shrimp paste (bagoong alamang), making every bite a perfect balance of flavors.

Ingredients

The Meat and Base

  • 2 lbs oxtail (cut into 2-inch pieces)
  • 1 lb beef tripe (cleaned and sliced, optional) or beef chuck
  • 8 cups water (for boiling)
  • 3 tbsp cooking oil
  • 1 medium onion (chopped)
  • 4 cloves garlic (minced)

The Sauce

  • 1 cup peanut butter (unsweetened, or ground roasted peanuts)
  • 1/4 cup toasted rice flour (dissolved in 1/2 cup water for thickening)
  • 2 tbsp annatto seeds (atsuete) soaked in 3 tbsp warm water (for color)
  • 3 cups reserved beef broth (from boiling the meat)

The Vegetables

  • 1 banana blossom (puso ng saging, sliced and soaked in salted water)
  • 1 bundle string beans (sitaw, cut into 2-inch pieces)
  • 2 medium eggplants (talong, sliced diagonally)
  • 1 bundle bok choy (pechay, ends trimmed)

Seasoning and Accompaniment

  • Salt and freshly cracked black pepper to taste
  • 1/2 cup sautéed shrimp paste (bagoong alamang), for serving

Procedure

  1. Tenderize the meat: In a large heavy-bottomed pot, bring the water to a boil. Add the oxtail and tripe (if using). Lower the heat to a simmer, cover, and cook for 2.5 to 3 hours, or until the meat is very tender. Skim off any scum that rises to the top. Once tender, remove the meat and reserve 3 cups of the cooking broth.
  2. Sauté the aromatics: In a separate large pot or Dutch oven, heat the cooking oil over medium heat. Add the chopped onions and garlic. Sauté until the onions are soft and translucent.
  3. Start the stew: Return the tenderized oxtail and tripe to the pot and sauté for a few minutes with the aromatics. Pour in the reserved 3 cups of beef broth and bring the mixture to a simmer.
  4. Create the peanut base: Stir in the peanut butter until it completely dissolves into the broth. Squeeze the soaked annatto seeds to extract their bright red-orange color, discard the seeds, and pour the colored water into the pot. Let it simmer for 5 to 10 minutes.
  5. Thicken the sauce: Gradually pour in the dissolved toasted rice flour mixture while stirring continuously. Let it simmer for another 5 minutes, allowing the sauce to become rich and thick.
  6. Cook the vegetables: Add the banana blossoms, string beans, and eggplants. Cover and simmer for about 5 to 7 minutes, or until the vegetables are tender but still hold their shape.
  7. Add the finishing touches: Gently fold in the fresh bok choy. Let it cook for a final 2 to 3 minutes until the leaves are just wilted and bright green.
  8. Season and serve: Taste the peanut sauce and lightly season with salt and pepper as needed (remember, the shrimp paste will add a lot of saltiness later). Transfer to a large serving bowl and serve piping hot with steamed white rice and a generous side of sautéed shrimp paste (bagoong).
